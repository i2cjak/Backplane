# T3Code parity

What `docs/reference/t3code-parity.md` describes, and where the Bend
Backplane stands. Updated as features land.

## Done

- **Threads:** per-project threads, sidebar order, Settled after N days (setting, 1–90), pin, archive, rename, delete. Delegated child threads are shown under their parent.
- **Sidebar:** sections pinned → active → snoozed → settled, then an Archived shelf per project (unarchive from the thread's header). Snooze for 1 hour, until 9:00 tomorrow (the server's time zone) or 1 week; a snoozed thread wakes by the clock alone and never auto-settles while snoozed (`settle_never_snoozed`, `snooze_wakes`). Manual order of pinned and active threads by drag (native) or move buttons (web), with binary fractional order keys (`key_mid_above`, `key_mid_below`, `key_after`). Projects reorder by dragging their names (native and web): dragged projects sit first by their keys, the rest by latest activity (`project_keyed_first`, `project_unkeyed_below`). The row's dot shows approval > input > working > failed > queued > ready (`status_*`).
- **Event log:** the log, replay, and a local store (`events.jsonl`); `replay_snoc` and `resume_is_replay` are laws.
- **Providers:**
  - Claude (stream-json, long-lived, restarted when model or mode changes)
  - Codex (`exec --json`, per turn, resumed)
  - Grok (ACP, per turn, resumed)
  - Muse Code (MSP: `muse serve`, per turn, resumed with `session/resume`; `src/core/msp.bend`)
  - Google Antigravity, as T3 Code does it: Google's own agent (`agy_acp_server` from the ACP registry, pinned by version, size and SHA-256, installed from Settings into `<home>/antigravity`), ACP per turn (authenticate with the chosen method, `session/new` or `session/resume`, `session/set_mode`, `session/set_config_option` for the model; `src/core/agy.bend`). The agent signs in by itself (Gemini API key by default, Vertex AI, Gemini Enterprise, or a Google account, which Settings warns Google's terms may punish); Backplane never reads a Google token, and the way back from Google's page can be pasted from another device. Several accounts are profiles (`agy.account`).
  - oh-my-pi (`omp acp`, ACP per turn, resumed by session id; `src/core/omp.bend`): installed by the person (`curl -fsSL https://omp.sh/install | sh`, or `bun install -g @oh-my-pi/pi-coding-agent`), then `omp login`. Backplane builds no sign-in and sends no key value. Models come from `omp models --json`; thinking levels are per model, from its catalog (a model without reasoning has none). Plan mode is not offered in v1.
- **Runtime modes:** runtime modes map to each provider's permissions. Plan mode is supported for Claude and Grok.
- **Approvals:** outside full access, Claude, Grok and Muse ask through Backplane: Allow / Allow for session / Deny. Grok's ACP `session/request_permission` waits parked for the answer (allow once, allow always, reject); Muse's `approval/request` gets its receipt at once and its `approval/decide` once answered (supervised is Muse's `promptUnmatched`, accept-edits and auto `onRequest`, where its own judge may approve, full access `allowAll` without the shell sandbox); a tool allowed for the session is allowed again at once. AskUserQuestion becomes a question with options. ExitPlanMode becomes a proposed plan with Implement / Dismiss.
- **oh-my-pi approvals:** Supervised runs `omp acp --approval-mode always-ask`; AcceptEdits and AutoMode run `write`; full access runs `yolo`. A write asks through `elicitation/create`; shell, edit, delete, and move ask through `session/request_permission` per omp's source (not yet seen in a live turn). Both paths expose only Allow and Deny; Allow is one-time. A form that is not an approval is declined and never asked. Older clients may still send `session`; the hub normalizes that answer to one-time `accept`, never a session-wide permission.
- **Queued turns:** a message sent while a turn runs is stored on the server and starts when that turn ends. Resends are stored once (`resend_stored_once`).
- **Queue and steer (orchestrator V2):** each waiting message is its own item above the composer (native, web, Android, iOS) with Steer (Send when no turn runs), Edit (back into the composer), Up and Remove; a held queue (after a stop or a restart) also offers Resume. A message sent while a turn runs is queued or steers the turn by the `followup` setting (Settings → Threads; unset: Claude steers, Codex, Grok and Muse queue); ctrl+Enter (a long press on phones) does the other. Claude takes a steer on its input; a Codex, Grok or Muse turn stops and starts again on the same session with the steering message (`follow_*`, `queue_*`, `steer_*`). Events `QueueEdited`, `QueueRemoved`, `QueueMoved`, `QueueTakenOne`; RPCs `queue.edit/remove/move/steer`, and `turn.start` takes `mode` (`auto`, `queue`, `steer`).
- **Restarts and stops (orchestrator V2):** a hub that starts from its log ends every turn still marked running as interrupted and closes every open ask, since the process and the parked request died with the old hub; a delegated child's task fails and its parent hears of it (`recover_*`). Queued messages stay held until `queue.resume` or the next message. Stop kills a Codex or Grok turn's process (Claude takes an interrupt on its input); each turn process's output is tagged with its pid, so a stopped one can never end the next turn (`interrupt_*`, `stopped_turn_quiet`, `tag_*`). An open ask makes its thread pending and a running child task keeps its parent from settling (`ask_makes_pending`, `answer_clears_pending`, `settle_waits_for_children`).
- **Forks and handed-over context (orchestrator V2):** Fork (thread header, every client) makes a new thread that goes on from the current one: it records its source (`ThreadForked`), works in its own worktree made from the source's last checkpoint, and carries the source's conversation as handed-over context, queued ahead of its first message. Bring back (on a fork) queues the fork's conversation into its source for the source's next message; the fork's files stay on its branch. Handed-over context (`src/core/handoff.bend`) is a budgeted selection, never a summary: short threads go whole, longer ones keep the newest items within 16,000 characters plus the first request and say how many were left out (read them with `thread_read`); one item over 4,000 characters is cut and says so. It shows above the composer as a Context row that can be removed, never runs a turn by itself, and never shortens the user's own message (`hand_*`, `handoff_*`, `fork_*`, `back_needs_fork`). Test: `test/handoff_test.bend`.
- **Switching providers (orchestrator V2):** between turns a thread can go on with another provider: the picker's provider chips (window, web) or its Provider section (phones), RPC `thread.provider`, event `ProviderSet`. Each provider keeps its own session of the thread and how much of it it saw (`session.<thread>.<provider>`, `seen.<thread>.<provider>`): coming back resumes its own conversation and it is handed only what it missed; a provider new to the thread is handed it all (the same budgeted selection as forks). A running turn is never switched (`switch_*`).
- **Todo lists (orchestrator V2):** an agent's plan for the run shows above the composer as "Todo 2/5" with a line per step ([x] done, [>] being done, [ ] to do) in every client, while the turn runs or a step is left (`src/core/todo.bend`, event `TodosSet`). Claude's task tools (TaskCreate/TaskUpdate, enabled with `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TODO_TOOLS=1`; TodoWrite on older versions) are folded by the hub, a created step named by its tool result; Codex's `todo_list` items and Grok's ACP `plan` updates set the list whole (`todo_*`).
- **Context meter (orchestrator V2):** the model chip says how much of its model's window the thread's Claude session holds ("claude · opus · ctx 12% of 200k"), in every client. Each model call's usage (input, both caches, output) is a reading, the result line names the window, a compaction says what is left; `UsageSet` goes to the log only when a reading changes (`usage_*`). Codex reports only a turn's summed usage and no window, so its threads show none yet.
- **Drafts:** every keystroke's draft is written at once (native: `<home>/drafts/<thread>`; web: localStorage; phones: preferences) and read back at start, so a crash loses nothing typed (`draft_*`).
- **Thread inboxes:** threads message each other through inboxes, and links between threads are shown (`inbox_never_interrupts`).
- **Orchestration:** delegate_task, task_status, task_cancel, thread_list, thread_read, thread_send and orchestrator_capabilities over MCP. Delegation is bounded and each result is delivered once (laws). Orchestrator V2's thread tools too: `thread_launch` (a top-level thread in the caller's project with its first message, any provider), `thread_update` (rename, or regenerate the title from the first message), `thread_wait` (until the thread is done: no turn running, nothing queued; the hub answers at once and the server asks again each second, 1 s to 10 min), `thread_interrupt`, and `thread_send` with a mode (auto, queue, steer). All stay within the caller's project (`mcp_*`, `wait_*`).
- **Subagents:** one panel in every client lists current work first, then work needing the person and recent failures. Other finished rows sit behind a closed "N done" toggle; finished rows with known end times leave the panel after 24 hours. A Claude subagent shows its prompt's first line and newest steps (tools and what it wrote), and a delegated task or launched row opens its thread. Tasks and launched threads share model labels ("opus", "launched · opus"). The head opens or shuts the panel per thread on this device; new work or newly needed attention reopens it, while finishing work leaves it shut. The web keeps that choice across reloads; the window keeps it during the session and reconnects. A subagent's own lines go to its log, not the thread's timeline. The panel says "N waiting · N at work" as needed, and a sidebar row says "N agents" where its age goes (`src/core/subs.bend`, `subs_*`).
- **Model picker:** per thread, plus a default for new threads. Models are detected from each installed CLI. In the window it opens above the composer at its left edge; every piece's place comes from `src/core/menu.bend`, so however many models and effort levels a CLI reports, rows stack, chips flow onto new lines, names and labels keep their own columns, and the menu never goes above the thread's header (it scrolls instead) nor past the composer's width (laws `menu_*`). A press anywhere off it, or Esc, closes it (`menu_press_*`). The web client draws it as a two-column grid over a shade that closes it.
- **Project picker:** opens on the projects: this hub's own (Enter opens the latest thread, or starts one), then those it once had and those found on disk (a folder under home, five levels down, holding `.backplane` or `.backplane.json`; RPC `fs.find`), each added back in one step. The filter is fuzzy on names; a typed path (`~/…`, `/…`) is added, browsed or created. "Add a project from a folder" opens the folder browser (Esc goes back). The phones' picker opens the same way; typing `~/` browses. `src/core/client.bend` `Proj.*`, `src/mobile/picker.bend`.
- **Project badges:** each project has a colour and an icon (settings `project.color.<id>`, `project.icon.<id>`; without one, a colour from its id and the folder icon, laws `badge_*`). The sidebar shows the icon by the project's name, a click on it opens an editor of tints and icons under the name, the selected thread's row carries the colour down its left edge, and a thread's header starts with the icon.
- **Reasoning effort:** per thread (low, medium, high, xhigh; Grok up to high; Muse minimal to max), as chips in the model picker, kept in the settings map (`effort.<thread>`). Claude `--effort` (its process restarts when the effort changes), Codex `-c model_reasoning_effort`, Grok `--reasoning-effort`, Muse `turn/start`'s `reasoningEffort`.
- **Skills:** user (`~/.claude/skills`) and project (`<cwd>/.claude/skills`) skills from SKILL.md frontmatter (name, description, disable-model-invocation, user-invocable; the user's win a clash). Claude reads them itself; Codex, Grok and Muse get a skills block in their instructions. A `$skill` mention becomes `/skill` for Claude and an instruction to read its SKILL.md for the others. The composer offers skills as you type `$` (Tab or Enter completes).
- **System prompt:**
  - runtime block
  - KiCad runtime, with fork IPC when the setting allows it
  - canonical project files
  - KiStack skills, latest (a pinned fallback)
  - orchestration
  - browser tools
- **Git:**
  - worktree threads (`env: worktree`, or the `thread.env` setting)
  - a checkpoint per turn in hidden refs
  - a Diff view
  - Revert thread, Commit & push, Open PR (`gh`)
  - a worktree thread's temporary branch is renamed from its first message, and commit messages are written from the thread's diff (a one-shot run of the text generation model; the thread's title when that fails)
- **Thread titles:** a thread's first message titles it at once with its first line; the text generation model then writes a 3 to 7 word title, which replaces that only while the thread still has it (a rename by the user wins; no answer changes nothing). Local and worktree threads alike.
- **Text generation model:** the settings `text.provider` (`claude`, `codex`, `grok` or `muse`; default `claude`) and `text.model` (empty, or another provider's model: the provider's light model, `haiku`, `gpt-6-luna`, `grok-4.7-build-fast` or `muse-spark-1.3`) pick the model for all one-shot writing: thread titles, branch names and commit messages. Claude runs `claude -p` without tools, Codex `codex exec --json` in a read-only sandbox at low effort, Grok its headless single-turn mode, Muse `muse exec` with no tools and one model step.
- **Terminal:** a shell per thread in a pty (ctrl+j), rendered by the Bend VT emulator in both clients' state. The web client draws it too: styled rows, the cursor, keys and paste while focused, a Terminal button and ctrl+j, and a phone's keyboard.
- **Diff on the web:** the web client's Diff button and ctrl+d show what the thread changed: files, hunks, and added and removed lines. It has Commit & push, Open PR and Revert thread, and fills the screen on a phone.
- **Attachments:** files and images in a turn.
  - The web client attaches through Attach (a file input) or by pasting an image. It sends files in pieces (`attach.put`).
  - The native window attaches through Attach or the palette's "Attach a file" (a path field). Pasting `file://` URIs or image paths also attaches (`attach.file`).
  - The server stores each file under `<home>/attachments/<thread>/<key>-<name>`. It caps files at 10 MB, cleans names (law `attach_clean_ok`), and never writes outside that directory.
  - A message carries its attachments as trailing `[attachment: /abs/path]` lines (`src/core/attach.bend`). `MessagePosted` keeps its shape, and both clients draw these lines as chips.
  - Claude gets images as base64 content blocks in stream-json (the attachments directory is also an `--add-dir`). Codex gets `--image` flags. Grok and Muse get the path in the text, since Grok's ACP `promptCapabilities.image` is false.
  - The native window shows chips, not image thumbnails.
- **Keybindings:** ctrl+j terminal, ctrl+d diff, ctrl+n new thread, ctrl+shift+[ / ] previous / next thread, ctrl+shift+s settle, ctrl+shift+p pin, ctrl+k command palette, ctrl+1..9 jump to the Nth visible thread, ctrl+shift+f thread search (titles and messages), ctrl+p file picker (inserts `@path`).
- **Auto-update:** from GitHub releases with checksums, then a swap and restart. There is also an installer.
  - The `update.channel` setting is `stable` or `nightly`, and both clients have a Check now button.
  - Stable takes only full releases. Nightly also takes the `vX.Y.Z-nightly.YYYYMMDD.N` prereleases that `.github/workflows/nightly.yml` publishes daily through release.yml's jobs.
  - Semver orders nightlies by day and then by build, with every nightly before its release (laws `semver_pre_before_release`, `semver_nightly_later`, `semver_nightly_next_day`).
- **Remote:** a web client for other devices over Tailscale with a pairing token. It uses CBOR frames, resumes from a sequence number, and has an outbox, an app shell cache and gzip.
- **Machines:** each hub listens on the tailnet by default (`--no-tailscale` turns this off) and lets in its owner's devices without the token, going by `tailscale whois` (law `tailnet_no_owner_no_trust`). It finds the owner's other hubs with GET /hello, plus any listed in `BACKPLANE_PEERS`. The sidebar lists them: the native window switches its connection there through a WebSocket link, and the web client opens that machine's page. Board viewers still read files on the machine the window runs on.
- **Models:** the pickers show what each CLI offers now. Claude Code's /model list comes from an `initialize` control request, which calls no model. Codex's list comes from its cache, Grok's from `grok models` and Muse's from `model/list` on a `muse serve` that is started and ended at once. The lists are read again every 15 minutes and pushed to clients. The effort chips follow the model.
- **Viewers:** board, schematic and 3D (KiCad), STEP (through step2glb), and the agents' browser (Chrome). Clicking inspects an item. 3D moves like SolidWorks: drag (left or middle) turns the model about its centre with no up lock, ctrl pans, shift zooms, alt rolls, the wheel zooms at the pointer (forward out), 1-7 are the standard views, arrows turn 15 degrees (90 with shift), f fits. A settled view is drawn with four samples a pixel.
- **Board viewer on phones:** a thread's board, schematic or 3D model, live, on the GPU (Metal, GLES 3). The hub sends chunks once, as CBOR, then only the ones a save changed; the 5.6 MB KiCad "video" demo board arrives as 528 KB, and a moved track as one ~1 KB delta. Tapping inspects an item (net, ref, value, footprint...) with Mention in chat; 3D shows the board's body and parts with its copper, mask and silk on the faces.
- **The iOS app:** what the web client's thread view has: approvals, questions and plans to answer; runs of tool calls folded; links between threads, subagents and the thread that delegated; attachments from Photos or Files (sent in pieces) and images in replies, zoomable; `$skill` completion; the diff with Commit & push, Open PR and Revert; the thread's terminal; snooze, delete and unarchive; thread search and the file picker; the archived shelf; each row's status dot; the hub's settings. Android draws the new rows plainly for now.
